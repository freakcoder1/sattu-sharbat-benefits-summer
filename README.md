### What Is Sattu Sharbat?
Sattu sharbat is a traditional drink from Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, and surrounding regions. It is made by mixing sattu flour (roasted black chickpea) with water or buttermilk, then adding lemon juice, black salt, roasted cumin powder, and sometimes mint or ginger.

### Why It Is Popular in Summer
In traditional Indian cooking, summer calls for foods that are cooling, hydrating, and easy to digest. Sattu sharbat fits all three:

- **Hydration:** The drink encourages fluid intake when people lose more water through sweat
- **Electrolytes:** Black salt and lemon provide minerals lost through sweat
- **Sustained energy:** The complex carbohydrates in sattu provide steady energy rather than the crash from sugary drinks

### Nutritional Benefits
Per 100g, chickpea-based sattu provides approximately 20-25g protein, 5-8g fiber, 4-5mg iron, and 120-140mg magnesium. A glass of sattu sharbat (about 2-3 tablespoons of sattu) delivers roughly 6-7g of protein â€” more than a glass of milk.

The fiber in sattu supports digestion, and the low glycemic index (35-45) means it does not cause sharp blood sugar spikes.

### How to Make Sattu Sharbat
**Ingredients:**
- Sattu flour: 2-3 tablespoons
- Water or buttermilk: 1 glass
- Lemon juice: 1 teaspoon
- Black salt: to taste
- Roasted cumin powder: 1/2 teaspoon
- Mint leaves: a few (optional)
- Ginger: a small piece, grated (optional)

**Method:**
1. Mix sattu flour with a little water to form a smooth paste (no lumps)
2. Add the remaining water or buttermilk and stir well
3. Add lemon juice, black salt, cumin powder, mint, and ginger
4. Serve chilled

### A Healthier Alternative to Packaged Drinks
During summer, many people reach for packaged energy drinks, sugary sodas, or sports drinks. These are high in added sugar and provide little nutritional value. Sattu sharbat is a whole-food alternative that provides protein, fiber, and minerals without added sugar.

### Who Should Avoid It
People with chickpea allergies, diagnosed kidney disease requiring protein restriction, or IBS triggered by high-FODMAP foods should consult a doctor before adding sattu regularly.
